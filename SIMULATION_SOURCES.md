# Simulation source findings

NASA NTRS, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160000593 (accessed 14 November 2026): BASS-II used thin/thick flat samples, acrylic slabs, spheres and cylinders in a wind tunnel aboard ISS. The abstract reports flow speeds up to 53 cm/s, opposed/concurrent configurations and sensitivity to ventilation in the 0–5 cm/s range. Some reported flames could persist below 1 cm/s and flare up when airflow increased. These are experiment-specific observations, not universal thresholds or a calibration of the proposed browser model.

Official challenge locator: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/. Search identified the title “Flame in Freefall: SI-Powered Fire Safety Insights from Microgravity Combustion Data.” Plain extraction returned only the site's third-party-link notice, so the complete challenge text is not yet verified and is being read in the browser. Do not treat search snippets as confirmed requirements.

NASA Science, https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/resources/explainers-infographics/why-nasa-is-studying-flames-in-space/ (4 October 2024; accessed 14 November 2026): terrestrial candle shape is driven by buoyant circulation, while the article illustrates a spherical microgravity candle flame. It describes CIR, SoFIE and Saffire; it does not give a browser-ready universal combustion model or material-specific calibration.

NASA SVS, https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/31394/ (6 May 2026; accessed 14 November 2026): the SoFIE thin-fabric illustration describes fuel-vaporization-induced flow even in a quiescent microgravity chamber and repeated brightening/dimming with oxygen/fuel transport. Therefore “all microgravity flames are spherical” would be wrong, and a simplified 2D point-source transport demonstration must not be presented as a reproduction of BASS/SIBAL solid-fuel experiments. Any normalized model activity or flame colors are synthetic outputs, not measured heat-release, temperature or soot values.

The complete official challenge text was read from the loaded page on 14 November 2026. It asks for a web-based SI-powered dashboard that summarizes, ranks and interprets combustion investigations. Extracting data that supports predictive modeling is an optional user capability, not a provided universal simulator or a demand to invent material flammability predictions. It describes absent buoyancy, oxygen/airflow dependence and solid materials such as fabrics/plastics; it explicitly excludes rocket/propellant fire safety from the PSI research scope. The page says no partial-gravity combustion data is currently available in PSI, so intermediate-buoyancy controls must be labelled exploratory, not NASA-validated lunar/Martian predictions.
